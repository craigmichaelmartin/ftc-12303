FTC 12303 · 2026–27 BIOBUZZ presented by RTX

Tip the HIVE early, tip it often, then park.

Our robot takes POLLEN in at the front and shoots it out of the back with a fixed shooting wheel, so it aims by turning its back to the target. That suits a game decided by HIVE TIPS. This page lays out how to play it in AUTO, TELEOP and the last minute, which parts of the robot to tune, and what to test first. It's grounded in the TU05 manual, the official field setup guide, the Q&A board and early-season match data.

Strategy highlights

  • Win with TIPS. Each TIP is worth 20 points, and TIPS decide matches.
  • AUTO: our 4 preloads tip the start cell, then we leave and park. That's 28 points, or 36 and the SWARM RP with a partner that also leaves and parks.
  • TELEOP: the two-zone loop, one turn per cycle. Shoot out of the back, turn around once, sweep the spill intake-first and keep driving to the other end, where the back already faces the new up cell. Two loads of 4 make each TIP after the first.
  • Last minute: fire what's on board at about 0:12, then both robots park. POLLEN only until NECTAR passes its tests.

Robot and code highlights

  • Pedro Pathing 3 drives the AUTO paths, on goBILDA Pinpoint odometry with X/Y pods.
  • Fixed rear shooter: the robot aims by turning its back to the cell opening. The driver holds RB to turn it there, and AUTO turns itself. The Limelight 3A looks out of the back too: its AprilTags correct the aim, and position comes from odometry, because the HIVE tags move when it tips.
  • One carrier wheel holds the balls and feeds them into the shooting wheel one at a time, only when the flywheel is at speed and the robot is aimed. To tune: the fixed exit angle, the feed time and a steel flywheel.
  • Never more than 4 balls: a sensor at the mouth counts every ball in, stops the intake at 4 and refuses NECTAR. Without it, the drivers count by eye and the tunnel must not fit a 5th ball.
  • One INIT menu sets alliance, start, routine, park slot and delay. Laptop tests sweep every AUTO route on both alliances.
The plan on one screen

Three phases, one job each

AUTO · 2:30 → 2:00

Fire the preloads, tip once, leave, park

  • Start at S1: front (intake) against our start wall (red: audience wall; blue: far wall), square, with the back toward our up cell.
  • Fire all 4 preloads. Three should tip it; the fourth is insurance against a bounce-out (it may also shorten the wait, but that is weak evidence).
  • Hold at S1 until the spill has landed, then take the corridor to our park slot: inside the LOADING ZONE, off the wall.
  • 28 points for us. With a partner that leaves and parks: 36 and the SWARM RP.
TELEOP · 2:00 → 1:00

Run the two-zone loop

  • The up cell faces its own end wall, so only one half of the field can score it.
  • After every TIP, the target swaps ends and the spill lands where we just were.
  • Loop: shoot out of the back → turn around once → sweep the spill intake-first → keep going to the other zone, where the back already faces the new up cell → shoot. Two loads of 4 make one TIP.
  • On the human player's "TIP!", the operator presses Y: our camera can't see our own TIP from the zone we shot from.
  • Goal: a load every 15–20 s and 2–3 TELEOP TIPS from us per match.
LAST MINUTE · 1:00 → 0:00

Keep tipping, dump the load, both robots park

  • The human player drops all remaining NECTAR into our LOADING ZONE when the field timer shows 1:00. The chime is only a courtesy; the coach says "NECTAR, GO" at 0:59.
  • Plan the last cycle to end in the half next to our LOADING ZONE (red: far half; blue: audience half), about 3 s from it.
  • On the coach's "0:12, last volley", fire whatever is on board (2 points per ball left in the up cell), then park. If that volley tips the HIVE, let the spill land first, or drive toward our alliance wall before crossing to the LOADING ZONE (G409).
  • Flowers are a phase-3 option, only if NECTAR placement works.
Why this plan. Early events are low scoring: the median alliance scored 35 points, and alliances averaged 0.9 TIPS per match (44% scored none). At the Saline preview in Michigan, the winning alliance averaged about 3.7 TIPS and over 100 points. One reliable AUTO TIP plus two or three TELEOP TIPS from our robot puts us with the top Michigan teams.

Timing budget (estimates to beat in practice)

0 s102030

The first ball leaves about 0.3–0.6 s after START, once the flywheel is at speed. The carrier pushes one ball per spin and stops as soon as the shot is confirmed (a flywheel speed dip or the feed beam); the next push waits at least 0.15 s, and until the flywheel is back at speed and the robot is aimed. An unconfirmed push stops after 0.4 s and waits 0.25 s more, so 4 balls take about 1.5 s when every shot is confirmed and about 3 s when none is. The HIVE can sit 0.25–3.4 s after the threshold ball before it swings, then takes about 0.75 s to swing (up to about 2.2 s). That was measured at the Saline preview. Overloading the cell may shorten the wait (weak evidence); A1 fires all four mainly as insurance against bounce-outs.

The bars assume a tuned path follower. At the shipped 0.5 speed cap the drives take about twice as long (S1 → park about 5–6 s, garden → Zone B about 6.5–8 s), so A2 and A3 wait for tuning. Some limits are fixed times from START: every routine drops everything and parks at 23 s, A3 role 2 fires only if it saw TIP 1 by 9 s and leaves for its park slot by 13 s, and A3 role 1 stays out of Zone B until 17 s.

AUTO traps

  • Touching any wall at the end of AUTO loses LEAVE, even inside the LOADING ZONE (Q&A 21).
  • Stay on our half in AUTO (red: columns A–C, Y ≤ 0; blue: columns D–F, Y ≥ 0); interfering with the other side is a MAJOR foul (G402).
  • Stay out of the spill landing box until the balls have landed: the first can land about 1.2 s after the threshold ball, usually about 3 s, sometimes 6 s. Any deflection of a falling ball counts (G409, Q&A 17).
  • The ball count starts at the INIT Preloads value: set it to 4 every match. The intake must stop at 4 (G407, no momentary exception per Q&A 3 and 23). Without a mouth sensor nothing counts the A2 garden sweep, so the tunnel itself must stop a 5th ball (experiment 0a).
  • Set Park with the partner before the match. A robot in the inner slot blocks the corridor end, so the first robot to arrive takes the corner slot.
  • A3 role 2 starts on S6 only. If S6 is blocked, skip A3: from S7 the robot would have to turn about 31° with its front against the wall, so the code won't run role 2 there and the robot sits still.
  • The human player may enter the NECTAR unlocked by an AUTO TIP during AUTO (Q&A 11).
TELEOP · 2:00 → 1:00

The two-zone loop

An empty cell needs 8 POLLEN, but a robot may hold only 4. So every TIP after the first takes two loads, from us, our partner, or both. The spill from a TIP lands in the zone we shot from, and the next up cell faces the other way. That makes the loop: shoot, sweep the spill, cross, shoot. Our shooter points out of the back, so the spill lands behind the robot, between it and the HIVE. Turn around once, sweep the spill intake-first and keep driving the same way to the other zone: the robot arrives with its back already toward the new up cell. That is one turn per cycle.

Where the ammo comes from, in order

  1. Our own spill. It's predictable and lands in our zone, behind the robot, usually about 3 s after the threshold ball (up to about 6 s). The first spill also holds our 3 staged NECTAR: steer around it (only the mouth sensor can refuse NECTAR, and only on contact).
  2. Loose balls in our zone, from misses and partner spills.
  3. GARDEN POLLEN. Either garden is fair game in TELEOP (1 pt each if left, worth much more as ammo).
  4. Flower bottoms. 16 of the 24 non-preload POLLEN start inside flowers. Test whether our intake wheel can pull them through the 3.55 in retrieval opening.
  5. The opponent's spill, without herding or taking their NECTAR (G408, G411).

Pace and call-outs

  • Target: one 4-ball load every 15–20 s. That's a TIP every 30–40 s alone, or about 20 s with a shooting partner.
  • To shoot, stop and hold driver RB: the robot turns until its back points at the cell. The operator light is PURPLE until the robot is aimed and GREEN when it is ready to fire; the operator's RB then fires the load, each ball only when ready.
  • Call it out loud: "Four in. Eight, it's going." The second robot stops firing until the swing finishes. Launching at a moving HIVE can spoil the TIP. On the human player's "TIP!", the operator presses Y: our camera can't see our own TIP from the zone we shot from.
  • Two long buzzes mean the camera already switched the target, and a Y within 3 s then only confirms it (one strong short buzz). One long buzz means Y switched it. Pressed Y by mistake? Press it again.
  • After any switch, shoot from the end the up cell faces (stay if you're already there). A red operator light means the target cell faces away: wrong zone, or the HIVE tipped and nobody pressed Y.
  • The robot's 1 s pause after a target switch only retargets, and it cancels a queued volley (press RB again). It can't protect a tipping HIVE, so fire each load as one volley and start no new volley after "8, it's going".
  • Defense: blocking shots is legal (Q&A 2). Reposition inside the ±40° cone, and the driver holds RB to turn the back onto the cell from the new spot. With auto-aim the robot fires only while nearly still (under 6 in/s and 20°/s) and aimed, and doesn't hold its position, so a push pauses firing: re-aim with RB. Never pin longer than 3 s (G421).
  • Never control 5 balls or any opponent NECTAR. There is no momentary allowance. With the mouth sensor, a ball counts once it has passed the sensor, the intake stops at 4, and the intake wheel reverses for as long as NECTAR is at the mouth: always the opponent's, and ours too while ACCEPT_OWN_NECTAR = false (phase 1). The sensor only reacts on contact, so still steer around NECTAR. Without it (TeleOp INIT shows !!!! NO MOUTH SENSOR at the top), nothing counts balls in or refuses NECTAR. The count goes down by one per feed and never goes up by itself, so after a volley RT doesn't stop on its own (only the mouth sensor, or a slot-4 sensor that sees the 4th ball in the tunnel, stops it at 4): the driver stops intaking at 4 by eye. An eject (operator LB) doesn't lower the count: the operator presses back once per ball that went out, or the intake keeps refusing. The operator fixes the count with back (−1) and left-stick click (+1).

Robot · keep the design, tune it

Refinements, in priority order

The robot: a SWYFT mecanum drive with X/Y odometry pods on a goBILDA Pinpoint. At the front, a full-width intake wheel of star wheels on a floating arm, driven by a DC motor, pulls balls in over a clear ramp into a tunnel. A carrier wheel above the tunnel holds the balls while it is stopped and pushes one at a time into the shooting wheel when it spins. The shooting wheel is fixed at the top of the back, so the robot aims by turning its back to the target. A Limelight 3A camera (the only legal programmable vision coprocessor, R702) looks out of the back too. With the carrier on a motor, that is 7 motors (R503 allows 8), and no servos are needed. The design is right for this game. The list below is about making it legal, consistent and countable. Anything larger is marked optional.

If a servo turns the carrier, configure that port as a Continuous Rotation Servo named carrier.
tuneMount the Limelight on the back, looking out the same way as the shooter, pitched ~35–40° up; set its position and angles with Check: LimelightFrom the back it sees the target cell's tags while the robot aims. The HIVE tags face down from about 50 in high, and they move when the HIVE tips, so they're for aiming, not for finding the robot's position.
tuneIntake motor direction and speed (Check: Sensors, A = intake in)The intake wheel runs on a goBILDA Yellow Jacket motor on the floating front arm. Set INTAKE_DIRECTION so balls go in, then the INTAKE_IN_POWER that picks up fastest without knocking balls away, garden balls along the wall too (experiment 8).
tuneProtect the hubs and wiring, cradle the battery, guard the shooting wheelDefense is "everywhere in TELEOP" in Michigan. A ball landing on the robot near a spill risks G409. Inspection doesn't require a battery cradle, but a loose battery can get the robot disabled (G412.B).

Decisions

  • Pinpoint for position, AprilTags for aim. SDK 12.0 says BIOBUZZ tags move and are "not suitable for absolute Field Localization". Each tag cluster's origin is the center of the cell opening, which is exactly what we aim at. The camera is fixed to the chassis and looks out of the back, so it sees the target cell's tags while the robot aims; its bias corrects odometry heading error and is learned only while the robot is still.
  • Aim by turning the robot. The shooter is fixed, so a ball is fed only when the robot's heading is inside an aim tolerance. It is worked out for every shot from the 20 in opening and the distance from the robot's centre to the cell, because the robot turns about its centre and a heading error swings the shot about it. It is 4° (the cap) for every straight-on shot up to about 54 in and at every AUTO shooting spot, 3.2° for a 70 in shot and 2.5° for a 92 in shot (both straight on), and 1.8° for a 92 in shot 40° off-axis (40° to the side of head-on). Shot distances (on the AIM line, in the RPM table and for the 30–92 in band) are measured from where the ball leaves the robot once it is aimed, so they don't change while it turns. Driver RB turns the robot there and stops inside half the tolerance; in AUTO the robot turns itself during every volley whenever it is more than half the tolerance off. Operator d-pad ←/→ trims the aim by 0.5°.
  • Pedro Pathing 3 for AUTO paths. Its AutoTune is the fastest route to reliable paths once the odometry pods are on, but use it at home only: its Wi-Fi web server is illegal anywhere in an event venue (R704.D, Q&A 26). Until Foresight is tuned, paths are capped at half speed, which is why A2 and A3 wait for tuning.
  • Flywheel: start with the hub's setVelocity PIDF (F = 32767 / max ticks/s, P = 0.1·F, I = 0.1·P). Call it "ready" only after 3 loops within 2.5% or 60 rpm, whichever is larger.
  • Which cell is up: red starts with the audience cell up, blue with the far cell up. The operator presses Y on every TIP. The camera sees the up cell only from the zone it faces, so it confirms a TIP only after we reach the new zone with the back, and the camera, toward the cell (0.5 s of unbroken evidence); a Y within 3 s of a camera switch only confirms it.
  • Laptop tests: ./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest runs the JUnit tests with no robot: every AUTO leg swept for walls, FLOWERS, HIVE legs, partners and spill boxes, every start with the front against its wall and every shooting pose with the back on its cell, plus ball handling (carrier and intake), aim, camera geometry, the HIVE tracker and shot dips.

Competition hygiene

  • Panels, FTC Dashboard and Pedro AutoTune must be out of the build at events. Wi-Fi streaming is banned anywhere in the venue, pits included (R704.D, Q&A 26). Set PEDRO_AUTOTUNE = false in build.dependencies.gradle and re-deploy over USB before every event: every 12303 OpMode warns in INIT if AutoTune, Dashboard or Panels is still in the app. Tune the Limelight over USB too.
  • TeleOp INIT must not move anything during the 8 s transition (G403). AUTO INIT moves nothing either (G304.H).
  • 12303 Auto preselects 12303 TeleOp, so the drive team can't forget it.
  • Pose, ball count and HIVE state hand off from AUTO to TELEOP, so field-centric drive and aiming start correct. Don't restart the robot between them.
