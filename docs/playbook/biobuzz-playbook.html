FTC 12303 · 2026–27 BIOBUZZ presented by RTX

Tip the HIVE early, tip it often, then park.

Our robot is a turret pollen shooter, which suits a game decided by HIVE TIPS. This page lays out how to play it in AUTO, TELEOP and the last minute, which parts of the robot to tune, and what to test first. It's grounded in the TU05 manual, the official field setup guide, the Q&A board and early-season match data.

The plan on one screen

Three phases, one job each

AUTO · 2:30 → 2:00

Fire the preloads, tip once, leave, park

  • Start at S1: back on our start wall (red: audience wall; blue: far wall), square in front of our up cell.
  • Fire all 4 preloads. Three should tip it; the fourth is insurance against a bounce-out (it may also shorten the wait, but that is weak evidence).
  • Hold at S1 until the spill has landed, then take the corridor to our park slot: inside the LOADING ZONE, off the wall.
  • 28 points for us. With a partner that leaves and parks: 36 and the SWARM RP.
TELEOP · 2:00 → 1:00

Run the two-zone loop

  • The up cell faces its own end wall, so only one half of the field can score it.
  • After every TIP, the target swaps ends and the spill lands where we just were.
  • Loop: shoot → sweep the spill → cross → shoot. Two loads of 4 make one TIP.
  • On the human player's "TIP!", the operator presses Y: our camera can't see our own TIP from the zone we shot from.
  • Goal: a load every 15–20 s and 2–3 TELEOP TIPS from us per match.
LAST MINUTE · 1:00 → 0:00

Keep tipping, dump the load, both robots park

  • The human player drops all remaining NECTAR into our LOADING ZONE when the field timer shows 1:00. The chime is only a courtesy; the coach says "NECTAR, GO" at 0:59.
  • Plan the last cycle to end in the half next to our LOADING ZONE (red: far half; blue: audience half), about 3 s from it.
  • On the coach's "0:12, last volley", fire whatever is on board (2 points per ball left in the up cell), then park. If that volley tips the HIVE, let the spill land first, or drive toward our alliance wall before crossing to the LOADING ZONE (G409).
  • Flowers are a phase-3 option, only if NECTAR placement works.
Why this plan. Early events are low scoring: the median alliance scored 35 points, and alliances averaged 0.9 TIPS per match (44% scored none). At the Saline preview in Michigan, the winning alliance averaged about 3.7 TIPS and over 100 points. One reliable AUTO TIP plus two or three TELEOP TIPS from our robot puts us with the top Michigan teams.

The AUTO ladder

Build these in order. A rung goes to competition only after it works in 8 of 10 practice runs. Every routine is in the one OpMode 12303 Auto: the robot takes alliance, start spot, routine, park slot, start delay and preload count from a gamepad menu during INIT.

A0

Safe any start

Drive off the wall into the LOADING ZONE, to the Park slot set in INIT. Fallback when the shooter is down or a partner needs our start spot.

A1

Tip & Park primary

From S1, spin up and aim with odometry, refined by AprilTags, and fire the 4 preloads. Hold there until the spill has landed: the start cell's tags seen up and then gone for 1 s, or 6 s after the last shot. Then drive the corridor to our Park slot and stop off the wall. From S2–S4 it first drives to the Zone A side spot (red (55, −37); blue (−55, 37)) and fires there.

A2

Tip, Reload, Volley stretch

A1's volley, then no hold: sweep our 4 GARDEN POLLEN along our start wall, cross to B2 in Zone B and fire them into the second cell (still empty, so 4 can't tip it), then park in the Park slot. Alone that's 4 of the 8 needed for TIP 2. Needs a tuned path follower.

28+4 balls toward TIP 2
A3

Alliance double-tip needs a shooter partner

Robot 1 runs "A3 role 1": A2, but it waits at the corridor end until 17 s, shoots from the role-1 spot (red (−52, −30); blue (52, 30)) and parks in the inner slot. Robot 2 runs "A3 role 2" from S6, on the end wall opposite our S1 (red: far wall; blue: audience wall). Robot 1's 4 garden POLLEN complete the 8, for TIP 2 in AUTO. Both roles are in the 12303 Auto menu. Tuned robots only, after a rehearsal with the partner. Timing budget (estimates to beat in practice)

0 s102030

The first ball leaves about 0.3–0.6 s after START. Balls are at least 0.33 s apart (gate 0.18 s + 0.15 s), plus the flywheel's recovery, so 4 take about 1.5 s when every shot is confirmed and about 3 s when none is. The HIVE can sit 0.25–3.4 s after the threshold ball before it swings, then takes about 0.75 s to swing (up to about 2.2 s). That was measured at the Saline preview. Overloading the cell may shorten the wait (weak evidence); A1 fires all four mainly as insurance against bounce-outs.

The bars assume a tuned path follower. At the shipped 0.5 speed cap the drives take about twice as long (S1 → park about 5–6 s, garden → Zone B about 6.5–8 s), so A2 and A3 wait for tuning. Some limits are fixed times from START: every routine drops everything and parks at 23 s, A3 role 2 fires only if it saw TIP 1 by 9 s and leaves for its park slot by 13 s, and A3 role 1 stays out of Zone B until 17 s.

AUTO traps

  • Touching any wall at the end of AUTO loses LEAVE, even inside the LOADING ZONE (Q&A 21).
  • Stay on our half in AUTO (red: columns A–C, Y ≤ 0; blue: columns D–F, Y ≥ 0); interfering with the other side is a MAJOR foul (G402).
  • Stay out of the spill landing box until the balls have landed: the first can land about 1.2 s after the threshold ball, usually about 3 s, sometimes 6 s. Any deflection of a falling ball counts (G409, Q&A 17).
  • The ball count starts at the INIT Preloads value: set it to 4 every match. The intake must stop at 4 (G407, no momentary exception per Q&A 3 and 23).
  • Set Park with the partner before the match. A robot in the inner slot blocks the corridor end, so the first robot to arrive takes the corner slot.
  • If S6 is blocked, skip A3: from S7 the second cell is 31° off our turret centre, outside its ±28° limits (unless the turret travels more than ±32°, e.g. with the 5-turn servo).
  • The human player may enter the NECTAR unlocked by an AUTO TIP during AUTO (Q&A 11).
TELEOP · 2:00 → 1:00

The two-zone loop

An empty cell needs 8 POLLEN, but a robot may hold only 4. So every TIP after the first takes two loads, from us, our partner, or both. The spill from a TIP lands in the zone we shot from, and the next up cell faces the other way. That makes the loop: shoot, sweep the spill, cross, shoot.

  T -- "tipping" --> W["Hold fire. Operator: Y on TIP! Stay out of the spill box until the balls land"]

  T2 -- "not yet" --> R2["Reload near Zone B / loading zone"] --> B

Where the ammo comes from, in order

  1. Our own spill. It's predictable and lands in our zone, usually about 3 s after the threshold ball (up to about 6 s). The first spill also holds our 3 staged NECTAR: the intake refuses NECTAR, but steer around it anyway.
  2. Loose balls in our zone, from misses and partner spills.
  3. GARDEN POLLEN. Either garden is fair game in TELEOP (1 pt each if left, worth much more as ammo).
  4. Flower bottoms. 16 of the 24 non-preload POLLEN start inside flowers. Test whether our roller can pull through the 3.55 in retrieval opening.
  5. The opponent's spill, without herding or taking their NECTAR (G408, G411).

Pace and call-outs

  • Target: one 4-ball load every 15–20 s. That's a TIP every 30–40 s alone, or about 20 s with a shooting partner.
  • Call it out loud: "Four in. Eight, it's going." The second robot stops firing until the swing finishes. Launching at a moving HIVE can spoil the TIP. On the human player's "TIP!", the operator presses Y: our camera can't see our own TIP from the zone we shot from.
  • Two long buzzes mean the camera already switched the target, and a Y within 3 s then only confirms it (one strong short buzz). One long buzz means Y switched it. Pressed Y by mistake? Press it again.
  • After any switch, shoot from the end the up cell faces (stay if you're already there). A red operator light means the target cell faces away: wrong zone, or the HIVE tipped and nobody pressed Y.
  • The robot's 1 s pause after a target switch only retargets, and it cancels a queued volley (press RB again). It can't protect a tipping HIVE, so fire each load as one volley and start no new volley after "8, it's going".
  • Defense: blocking shots is legal (Q&A 2). Reposition inside the ±40° cone and use the turret to shoot from another spot. With auto-aim the robot fires only while nearly still (under 6 in/s and 20°/s) and doesn't hold its position, so a push pauses firing. Never pin longer than 3 s (G421).
  • Never control 5 balls or any opponent NECTAR. There is no momentary allowance. A ball counts once it has passed the mouth sensor, and the intake stops at 4. The roller reverses for as long as NECTAR is at the mouth: always the opponent's, and ours too while ACCEPT_OWN_NECTAR = false (phase 1). The sensor only reacts on contact, so still steer around NECTAR.

Phase-3 options, only if NECTAR tests pass

  • NECTAR as tip accelerant: 3 NECTAR + 3 POLLEN tips an empty cell, 6 balls instead of 8. Our 3 staged NECTAR spill out with our first tip, and the human player can add one more per tip (all remaining at 1:00).
  • Flowers: the first NECTAR in a flower gets +5. The top-most NECTAR owns the flower at 2 pts per element inside. A flower still holding its staged POLLEN is worth about 13 pts for one NECTAR (15 if 4 POLLEN count, e.g. after we add one). About 40% of flowers still do at match end, and almost nobody scores them yet.
  • The cost: the hood gap is set for 2.8 in POLLEN, and NECTAR is 3.6 in. Until the NECTAR tests pass, the intake refuses all NECTAR (ACCEPT_OWN_NECTAR = false). Test before committing.

Robot · keep the design, tune it

Refinements, in priority order

The robot: SWYFT mecanum drive with X/Y odometry pods, a turret flywheel shooter fed up through the hollow turret center, a full-width intake, and a Limelight 3A camera (the only legal programmable vision coprocessor, R702). The design is right for this game. The list below is about making it legal, consistent and countable. Anything larger is marked optional.

Decisions

  • Pinpoint for position, AprilTags for aim. SDK 12.0 says BIOBUZZ tags move and are "not suitable for absolute Field Localization". Each tag cluster's origin is the center of the cell opening, which is exactly what we aim at.
  • Pedro Pathing 3 for AUTO paths. Its AutoTune is the fastest route to reliable paths once the odometry pods are on, but use it at home only: its Wi-Fi web server is illegal anywhere in an event venue (R704.D, Q&A 26). Until Foresight is tuned, paths are capped at half speed, which is why A2 and A3 wait for tuning.
  • Flywheel: start with the hub's setVelocity PIDF (F = 32767 / max ticks/s, P = 0.1·F, I = 0.1·P). Call it "ready" only after 3 loops within 2.5% or 60 rpm, whichever is larger.
  • Which cell is up: red starts with the audience cell up, blue with the far cell up. The operator presses Y on every TIP. The camera sees the up cell only from the zone it faces, so it confirms a TIP only after we reach the new zone (0.5 s of unbroken evidence); a Y within 3 s of a camera switch only confirms it.
  • Laptop tests: ./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest runs the JUnit tests with no robot: every AUTO leg swept for walls, FLOWERS, HIVE legs, partners and spill boxes, plus ball handling, the HIVE tracker and shot dips.

Competition hygiene

  • Panels, FTC Dashboard and Pedro AutoTune must be out of the build at events. Wi-Fi streaming is banned anywhere in the venue, pits included (R704.D, Q&A 26). Set PEDRO_AUTOTUNE = false in build.dependencies.gradle and re-deploy over USB before every event: every 12303 OpMode warns in INIT if AutoTune, Dashboard or Panels is still in the app. Tune the Limelight over USB too.
  • TeleOp INIT must not move anything during the 8 s transition (G403). AUTO INIT holds only the turret, the feeder gate and the hood (G304.H).
  • 12303 Auto preselects 12303 TeleOp, so the drive team can't forget it.
  • Pose, ball count and HIVE state hand off from AUTO to TELEOP, so field-centric drive and aiming start correct. Don't restart the robot between them.

Loading code onto the robot

  1. Once per laptop: Android Studio (Narwhal 3 Feature Drop or newer), then File › New › Project from Version Control with github.com/craigmichaelmartin/ftc-12303.git (the coach grants access).
  2. Once per robot: Driver Station app 12.0, the same version as the code. A Robot Configuration with the exact names from the README. The Limelight pipeline, set on the camera over USB at limelight.local:5801.
  3. Every time: pull the latest code, plug the laptop into the Control Hub's USB-C port, pick TeamCode and press Run.
  4. At events: USB only, and Pedro AutoTune out of the build.
