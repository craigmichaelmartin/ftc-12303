FTC 12303 · 2026–27 BIOBUZZ presented by RTX · playbook v1, Oct 20 2026

Tip the HIVE early, tip it often, then park.

Our robot is a turret pollen shooter, which suits a game decided by HIVE TIPS. This page lays out how to play it in AUTO, TELEOP and the last minute, which parts of the robot to tune, and what to test first. It's grounded in the TU05 manual, the official field setup guide, the Q&A board and early-season match data.

The plan on one screen

Three phases, one job each

AUTO · 2:30 → 2:00

Fire the preloads, tip once, leave, park

  • Start on the audience wall, square in front of our up cell (pose S1).
  • Fire all 4 preloads. Three tip it; the fourth makes the tip happen sooner.
  • Stay clear while the spill lands, then end inside our LOADING ZONE and off the wall.
  • 28 points for us. With a partner that leaves and parks: 36 and the SWARM RP.
TELEOP · 2:00 → 1:00

Run the two-zone loop

  • The up cell faces its own end wall, so only one half of the field can score it.
  • After every TIP, the target swaps ends and the spill lands where we just were.
  • Loop: shoot → sweep the spill → cross → shoot. Two loads of 4 make one TIP.
  • Goal: a load every 15–20 s and 2–3 TELEOP TIPS from us per match.
LAST MINUTE · 1:00 → 0:00

Keep tipping, dump the load, both robots park

  • The human player drops all remaining NECTAR into our LOADING ZONE at the 1:00 chime.
  • Plan the last cycle to end on the far side, 3 s from the LOADING ZONE.
  • At about 0:12, fire whatever is on board (2 points per ball left in the up cell), then park.
  • Flowers are a phase-3 option, only if NECTAR placement works.
Why this plan. Early events are low scoring: the median alliance scored 35 points with 0.9 TIPS. At the Saline preview in Michigan, the winning alliance averaged about 3.7 TIPS and over 100 points. One reliable AUTO TIP plus two or three TELEOP TIPS from our robot puts us with the top Michigan teams.

The AUTO ladder

Timing budget (estimates to beat in practice)

0 s102030

The HIVE can sit 0.25–3.4 s after the threshold ball before it swings, then takes about 0.75 s to swing. That was measured at the Saline preview. Overloading the cell shortens the wait, which is why A1 fires all four.

Partner script (say it at the field)

  1. "We start at the audience wall and tip our cell with preloads in the first 5 seconds."
  2. "Can you LEAVE and end in the LOADING ZONE, off the wall? That makes 16 and the SWARM RP." Park poses: (−27, −56) and (−51, −56). Both are partly in the zone, about 6 in off the wall and 6 in apart.
  3. If they shoot: "Start on the far wall. After our tip, fire your 4 into the far cell and we'll bring 4 more" (A3).
  4. If they don't move in AUTO: "We'll run A1. Please don't start in front of the audience-wall spot."

AUTO traps

  • Touching any wall at the end of AUTO loses LEAVE, even inside the LOADING ZONE (Q&A 21).
  • Stay on our half (columns A–C) in AUTO; interfering with the other side is a MAJOR foul (G402).
  • Don't stand in the spill box for 3–6 s after the threshold ball. Any deflection of a falling ball counts (G409, Q&A 17).
  • The ball count starts at 4 (the preloads). The intake must stop at 4 (G407, no momentary exception per Q&A 3 and 23).
  • The human player may enter the NECTAR unlocked by an AUTO TIP during AUTO (Q&A 11).
TELEOP · 2:00 → 1:00

The two-zone loop

An empty cell needs 8 POLLEN, but a robot may hold only 4. So every TIP after the first takes two loads, from us, our partner, or both. The spill from a TIP lands in the zone we shot from, and the next up cell faces the other way. That makes the loop: shoot, sweep the spill, cross, shoot.

flowchart LR
  A["Zone A: fire 4 into the up cell"] --> T{"8 in? HIVE tipping"}
  T -- "not yet" --> R["Reload: loose balls, garden, flower bottoms"] --> A
  T -- "tipping" --> W["Hold fire. Stay out of the spill box 3-6 s"]
  W --> S["Sweep the spill (up to 4)"] --> C["Cross via corridor y = -40"]
  C --> B["Zone B: fire 4 into the new up cell"]
  B --> T2{"8 in? HIVE tipping"}
  T2 -- "tipping" --> W2["Hold fire, sweep spill in Zone B"] --> C2["Cross back"] --> A
  T2 -- "not yet" --> R2["Reload near Zone B / loading zone"] --> B

Where the ammo comes from, in order

  1. Our own spill. It's predictable and lands in our zone about 3 s after the threshold ball.
  2. Loose balls in our zone, from misses and partner spills.
  3. GARDEN POLLEN. Either garden is fair game in TELEOP (1 pt each if left, worth much more as ammo).
  4. Flower bottoms. 16 of the 24 non-preload POLLEN start inside flowers. Test whether our roller can pull through the 3.55 in retrieval opening.
  5. The opponent's spill, without herding or taking their NECTAR (G408, G411).

Pace and call-outs

  • Target: one 4-ball load every 15–20 s. That's a TIP every 30–40 s alone, or about 20 s with a shooting partner.
  • Call it out loud: "Four in. Eight, it's going." The second robot stops firing until the swing finishes. Launching at a moving HIVE can spoil the TIP.
  • Defense: blocking shots is legal (Q&A 2). Keep moving inside the ±40° cone, and use the turret to shoot from another spot. Never pin longer than 3 s (G421).
  • Never control 5 balls or any opponent NECTAR. There is no momentary allowance. The robot auto-stops the intake at 4 and reverses on blue NECTAR at the mouth.

Phase-3 options, only if NECTAR tests pass

  • NECTAR as tip accelerant: 3 NECTAR + 3 POLLEN tips an empty cell, 6 balls instead of 8. Our own 3 NECTAR come back out with every spill of our first cell.
  • Flowers: the first NECTAR in a flower gets +5. The top-most NECTAR owns the flower at 2 pts per element inside. A flower still holding its staged POLLEN is worth 13–15 pts for one NECTAR. About 40% of flowers still do at match end, and almost nobody scores them yet.
  • The cost: the hood gap is set for 2.8 in POLLEN, and NECTAR is 3.6 in. Test before committing.

Point check for one full match (our robot)

  • AUTO: TIP 20 + LEAVE 3 + PARK 5 = 28
  • TELEOP: 2–3 TIPS together with our partner ≈ 40–60 as an alliance share
  • End: PARK 5, plus balls left in the up cell (2 each)
  • Ranking points: WIN 3, SWARM 1 (16 LEAVE+PARK pts), POLLINATOR 1 at 4 alliance TIPS, POLLINATOR 2 at 7

Refinements, in priority order

The robot's direction is right: turret shooter, full-width intake, hollow turret center. The list below is about making it legal, consistent and countable. Anything larger is marked optional.

PriorityChangeWhy
mustConfirm the camera is a Limelight 3AThe only legal programmable vision coprocessor. The 3G is explicitly prohibited (R702).
mustMain power switch, robot signs, grounding strapInspection blockers (R603, R4xx), and ESD protection for the Limelight.
mustBall sensors: color/distance at the intake mouth, a "slot 4 full" sensor, a beam-break at the feedCount to 4 and stop the intake; refuse opponent NECTAR. G407 and G408 have no momentary exception.
tuneFlywheel velocity control, and feed only when back to speed (gate ±2–3% with a short dwell)Each shot slows the 72 mm wheel by about 18–19%. The 28-count encoder reads speed coarsely.
tuneAdd the goBILDA 60 mm steel flywheel (551 g·cm², made for the 72 mm Hogback)More stored energy means less speed drop per ball, so 4-ball volleys land the same.
tuneSet the hood for a ~64° actual exit angle; fight bounce-outsA fixed hood covers lip distances of about 27–90 in. One team measured 44% bounce-outs with a solid fixed hood, so test foam or compliant surfaces and spin.
tuneTurret: a goBILDA 5-turn Dual Mode Servo in the same mount, or a REV Through Bore Encoder; add a zero reference and hard stopsAs built, the 300° servo through the ring gives only about 55–75° of turret travel and no feedback.
tunePower servos at 6 V (REV Servo Hub or goBILDA Servo Power Injector)Hub servo ports are 5 V and share 2 A per pair, and a stalled servo draws 2–2.5 A.
tuneMount Pinpoint + 2 pods. Put the Limelight on the turret, pitched ~35–40° upThe HIVE tags face down from about 50 in high, and they move when the HIVE tips, so they're for aiming, not for finding the robot's position.
tuneProtect the hubs and wiring, cradle the battery, guard the turret's open center and the flywheelDefense is "everywhere in TELEOP" in Michigan. A ball landing on the robot near a spill risks G409.
optionalMove the intake to a spare DC motor (5203, 1150 rpm), keeping the same rollerA servo makes about 2–3 W against about 22 W for a motor. If pickup is the bottleneck, this is the fix.
optionalNECTAR path: a compliant or adjustable hood gapOnly after POLLEN cycles are reliable.

Decisions

  • Pinpoint for position, AprilTags for aim. SDK 12.0 says BIOBUZZ tags move and are "not suitable for absolute Field Localization". Each tag cluster's origin is the center of the cell opening, which is exactly what we aim at.
  • Pedro Pathing 3 for AUTO paths. Its AutoTune is the fastest route to reliable paths once the odometry pods are on.
  • Flywheel: start with the hub's setVelocity PIDF (F = 32767 / max ticks/s, P = 0.1·F, I = 0.1·P). Call it "ready" only after 3+ loops in tolerance.
  • Which cell is up: red starts with the audience cell up, blue with the far cell up. The robot flips its target on a confirmed TIP, from vision or the operator's button.

Competition hygiene

  • Panels and FTC Dashboard must be disabled at events. Wi-Fi streaming is banned anywhere in the venue, pits included (R704.D, Q&A 26). Deploy code over USB.
  • TeleOp INIT must not move anything during the 8 s transition (G403).
  • AUTO OpModes preselect the TeleOp, so the drive team can't forget it.
  • Pose hands off from AUTO to TELEOP, so field-centric drive and aiming start correct.
